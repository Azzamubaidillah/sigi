# Coal at a year high and a weaker rupiah keep exporters' rupiah revenue lifted

## The view

Sigi's [macro page](/macro) shows two readings that matter to a coal exporter whose price is in dollars and whose revenue is counted in rupiah:

- Australian Newcastle coal was 147.1 dollars a tonne in September 2026, the top of its range for the past year (106.3 to 147.1), up from 135.2 in August and 38% above September 2025 [H, World Bank Pink Sheet].
- The rupiah was 18,069 to the dollar on 23 October, 8.6% weaker than a year earlier and near the weak end of the year's range (16,622 to 18,171) [H, Bank Indonesia JISDOR].

Taken together the rupiah price of coal is about half again as much as a year ago (1.38 times 1.09; the two readings are for different dates, so this is a rough figure) [L]. The view is that this lift to exporters' rupiah revenue lasts through the review date: coal stays above 125 dollars a tonne and the rupiah stays weaker than 17,500.

Both lines sit roughly halfway back from today's reading to the year's low.

## Who captures it

The register shows who holds the three listed coal producers named here, and so who receives most of what they earn [H]: Banpu Minerals (Singapore) holds 65.14% of [ITMG](/t/ITMG); the state holding company PT Mineral Industri Indonesia holds 65.93% of [PTBA](/t/PTBA); and Adaro Strategic Investments holds 41.10% of [AADI](/t/AADI). Their free floats are at most 29.47%, 34.07% and 37.70%.

ITMG's bound fell from 34.86% to 29.47% in September because a 5.39% position in its own name appeared in the register, probably shares it holds itself (an inference [L]). A holder of 5% or more is removed from the float by definition, so the move says nothing about trading.

## What would show it wrong

The two conditions above: the latest Newcastle reading below 125 dollars a tonne, or the rupiah stronger than 17,500 to the dollar. Coal is a monthly reading released early each month; the rupiah is a daily one.

## What I do not know

- Realised prices are not Newcastle's. Grades, contract terms and the domestic market obligation all sit between the benchmark and a company's revenue, and Sigi holds no company price, volume or cost data (C1). The view is about two inputs, not about earnings.
- Why the two readings moved. Brent crude jumped from 90.9 to 116.8 dollars a barrel in September, and a move in one energy price can pull another, but Sigi does not test that.
- Whether the three names are the right ones. They are listed coal producers whose controllers the register identifies; the list is not a ranking.
