# BRIS reaches the first float step through its rights issue

## The view

Bank Syariah Indonesia ([BRIS](/t/BRIS)) has a free float of at most 10.12%. Three state banks hold the rest of what the register can see: Mandiri 51.47%, BNI 23.24% and BRI 15.17%, together 89.88% [H]. The exchange's revised rule raises the minimum free float to 15% in steps. For a company with a market capitalisation of Rp5 trillion or more and a float under 12.5%, the first step is 12.5% by 31 March 2027 and the second is 15% by 31 March 2028 [M].

The view is that BRIS makes the first step on time, and that its second rights issue is how.

## What supports it

- BRIS plans to issue up to 6.8 billion new Series B shares. Its prospectus puts completion in the first quarter of 2027, after shareholders vote at an extraordinary meeting scheduled for 6 November 2026 [M, Kontan, 5 October 2026]. CNBC Indonesia reported on 15 October that one purpose is to meet the 15% float minimum [M].
- An analyst's calculation reported by Kontan: if Mandiri, BNI and BRI took no part, their combined stake would fall from about 90.1% to 78.5% and the float would rise from about 9.9% to about 21%, assuming every new share counts as free float [M]. That is well past 12.5%.
- The holders are state banks, and the final choice of scheme rests with the ultimate majority shareholder, Danantara [M, Kompas, 2 July 2026]. A decision by one owner is easier to date than a market outcome.
- On 23 October OJK told Kontan that banks below the line are still preparing corporate actions and that, in general, they show commitment to the schedule [M, Kontan, 26 October 2026]. Kontan counted 26 listed banks below 15% at 31 August: 10 due to reach 12.5% on 31 March 2027, two to reach 15% on that date, and 14 to reach 15% by 31 March 2029 [M].

## What would show it wrong

The two conditions above. The first is the plain test: a register dated 31 March 2027 or later that still shows BRIS's upper bound under 12.5%. Sigi's float figure is an upper bound, so a bound under 12.5% means the official figure is under 12.5% too.

The second is an early warning. If Mandiri's stake rises above 51.47%, the new shares are being absorbed by a holder instead of reaching the public.

## What I do not know

- Timing is the main risk. The issue is planned for the same quarter as the deadline, and the exercise price and the final schedule had not been announced when the analyst wrote on 30 September [M]. New shares that settle in April would not be in the register dated 31 March, and this thesis would be wrong on the date even if the float then rose.
- How much the state banks take up. A rights issue that Mandiri, BNI and BRI subscribe in full adds no free float.
- BRIS's capitalisation. Sigi stores no prices (C1). A third party put it at about Rp103 trillion on 19 December 2025 [L], which places BRIS among the large companies and sets the first deadline at 31 March 2027. If the exchange assigns another tranche, the dates move; the register figure does not.
- Which float figure counts. OJK's list gives 9.33% at 31 August, the company gave 9.91% in October, and Sigi's upper bound is 10.12%. They differ by definition and date. The conditions use Sigi's, and the official figure can be lower than it, never higher.
