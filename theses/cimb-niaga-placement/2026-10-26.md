# CIMB Niaga closes its float gap by placing shares, not by going private

## The view

One holder, CIMB Group Sdn Bhd, owns 91.45% of CIMB Niaga ([BNGA](/t/BNGA)), so the free float is at most 8.55% [H]. That figure has not moved in any register since February. OJK's own figure at 31 August, reported by Kontan, is 7.50% [M]; the official figure can be lower than Sigi's bound, never higher.

The exchange's rule raises the minimum to 15% in steps. For a company with a market capitalisation of Rp5 trillion or more and a float under 12.5%, the first step is 12.5% by 31 March 2027 [M]. Kontan counted 26 listed banks below 15% at 31 August: 10 due to reach 12.5% on that date, two to reach 15%, and 14 to reach 15% by 31 March 2029 [M].

The view is that CIMB Group closes the gap by putting shares into public hands, whether by a placement to strategic investors or by selling existing shares, and does not take the bank private.

## What supports it

- On 22 October CIMB Group said it had neither decided on nor formally proposed a privatisation of CIMB Niaga, called the bank an integral part of its regional operations, and said it remains committed to meeting all regulatory requirements, the minimum free float included, in a timely and structured way [M, Kontan, 26 October 2026].
- The bank's president director said it is assessing several options and is committed to following the rule [M, Kontan]. A Malaysian outlet, The Star, had reported three options under consideration: a placement to strategic investors, a new public offering and privatisation [M, as relayed by Kontan; I have not read the original].
- The first step is five months away and the parent has said it will meet it.

## What would show it wrong

The two conditions above. The first is the plain test: a register dated 31 March 2027 or later that still shows BNGA's upper bound under 12.5%. A bound under 12.5% means the official figure is under it too.

The second is an early warning of a take-private. If CIMB Group's stake rises above 91.45%, it is buying shares from the public, which is how a buy-out starts.

A delisting would remove BNGA from the register, and both checks would then read "unknown". Sigi cannot test it, so I would close this thesis in a new version with a note.

## What I do not know

- The route and the date. Nothing has been announced, and a placement that settles after 31 March would miss the register of that date.
- BNGA's tranche. Sigi stores no prices (C1). I take BNGA to be a large company, which sets the first step at 12.5% by 31 March 2027 [L]. If the exchange puts it in a later tranche, this thesis is judged against a date that does not apply and I will say so.
- Whether "not going private" is a view the facts can carry. A denial of a decision is not a decision, and the parent can change its mind.
